## 1. Mission

Build a customer-facing website + authenticated subscriber portal for
my market-intelligence operating system. The site has two jobs:

1. **Convince a sophisticated trader, in 90 seconds, that we are a
   serious research operation worth subscribing to.** Tone: calm,
   sober, audit-grade. NO marketing superlatives ("revolutionary",
   "AI-powered", "game-changing"). Speak the same language the brief
   itself speaks — regime, probability, calibration, context.

2. **Deliver our daily/weekly research artifacts (Daily Brief, Swing
   Brief, Yesterday Audit, Diagnosis Reports) to paying subscribers
   AND prove their accuracy with a live, sanitised outcome-log
   dashboard.** A prospect must be able to land on the site, browse
   one real (historical, redacted) sample brief in 60 seconds, and
   see our calibration on the last 90 trading days as a number, not
   a claim.

The website is the **front of house**. Everything that makes us
defensible — the detectors, the models, the feature engineering, the
calibration architecture — lives in the private engine repo
(`Project-freedom`) and is NEVER documented on the public site.

## 2. THE CRITICAL CONSTRAINT — what NEVER appears on this site

Read this section twice. It is the most important part of the prompt.

We compete in a space where any specific technique we publish gets
cloned into a competitor's stack within hours by anyone with an LLM
and a weekend. Therefore the public site **must communicate
sophistication without disclosing technique**. The website MUST NOT
contain any of the following, anywhere — not in copy, not in
tooltips, not in API responses, not in comments in the rendered HTML,
not in the open-source repo (the site repo will be public; treat it
as fully open):

**Forbidden surface — techniques, names, structures:**
- ❌ Names of any detector ("liquidity sweep", "stop run reclaim",
  "multi-bar imbalance", "premium/discount midpoint", "cumulative
  delta divergence", "volume-weighted swing", "order block",
  "fair value gap", "ORB", "EQH/EQL", "PWH/PWL", etc.). The
  brief surfaces "key levels", "supply pools", "demand pools",
  "regime tags" — those are fine because the customer needs them.
  The internal NAMES of how those are built are not.
- ❌ Names of any model ("PoolProximityModel", "UnifiedDirection",
  "PoolRespectModel", "Q model", "PolicyOutcomeModel",
  "PolicyReturnModel", "OptionsExpectedReturnModel",
  "SwingProximityModel"). On the site they are collectively
  "our proximity head", "our direction head", "our outcome model".
  No version numbers, no architecture, no LightGBM/XGBoost/neural
  references.
- ❌ Feature names ("path_efficiency_30", "direction_changes_30",
  "vol_regime_zscore_20d", "iv_percentile_60d", "dist_atr",
  "atr_at_last", any specific feature). On the site: "structural
  context", "regime features", "intraday state".
- ❌ Calibration architecture details ("isotonic regression",
  "bucket shrinkage", "distance calibration", "embargo bars",
  "purged walk-forward", "Huber loss with alpha=0.9",
  "winsorization at [-2.5, +5.0]"). On the site we say "every
  probability is calibrated against a hold-out window we never
  saw during fitting" — and stop there.
- ❌ Specific hyperparameters of any kind. No numbers like
  "min_atr=0.20", "reclaim_window=12", "swing_left=3".
- ❌ The detector/model hierarchy. No layer diagrams. No "L0-L7"
  stack mentions. No "we use 10-layer feature engineering".
- ❌ Specific OOS AUC / Spearman / calibration-error numbers from
  internal reports. We DO publish per-bucket hit rates from the
  outcome log (those are output-side and customer-verifiable). We
  do NOT publish model-internal validation metrics.
- ❌ The brain-dump / postmortem documents. None of those land on
  the public site. They live in the private repo only.
- ❌ Code snippets. No code on the public site, ever. (The
  customer portal can have a `/api/v1` reference IF we open one;
  for v1 we don't, customer integration is email + portal only.)
- ❌ Names of internal contributors / agents (Codex, Opus,
  Claude). The public face is the founder + brand name only.
- ❌ Any mention of which broker / data provider we use.
- ❌ Any mention of which cloud/infra/dependencies we run on.
- ❌ Source code attribution to AI tools, model identifiers, or
  any "generated by" notes.

**Allowed surface — output, discipline, philosophy, track record:**
- ✅ The brief OUTPUT (sample brief, real shape, real human prose).
  This is what we sell — the customer sees it anyway when they
  subscribe. Showing one historical one publicly costs us nothing.
- ✅ The renderer's tipster-guardrail policy — yes, brag about
  this. "We do not say buy or sell. We describe regime,
  probability, and audit." This IS a feature; competitors won't
  copy it because it's the harder discipline.
- ✅ The outcome-log calibration dashboard — per-bucket hit rate,
  calibration error over time, drift flags. This is the moat
  (compounding track record) AND it's customer-verifiable.
- ✅ The principle list: causality, no lookahead, fail-closed on
  data gaps, retrospective-vs-live disclosure, no tipster
  vocabulary. These describe DISCIPLINE, not TECHNIQUE.
- ✅ The product tiers: Daily Brief (intraday), Swing Brief
  (multi-day), Diagnosis (one-off audit), Audit Infrastructure
  (B2B for other quants). Tier names + scope + what a customer
  gets — yes. What's inside the engine that powers them — no.
- ✅ The Yesterday Audit feature, branded as a product
  differentiator: "every brief is followed by a public audit of
  yesterday's brief". This is huge for trust and we should lead
  with it.
- ✅ SEBI-safe-harbour language. Where appropriate, link the
  regulatory disclosures (these already exist in
  `docs/legal/` in the private repo — port them over).
- ✅ Indian market context — NSE indexes, NIFTY/BANKNIFTY,
  weekly options expiry cycle, IST sessions. We are an Indian
  market product; speak to Indian traders.

**Heuristic for any new content:** "If a competitor reads this line
and the only thing they can clone is the *aesthetic* of how we
deliver research, we're fine. If they can extract a technique, a
feature, an architecture, or a hyperparameter, the line gets cut."

If in doubt: leave it out.

## 4. Tech stack

Opinionated stack. Don't pick alternatives unless you have a strong
reason and surface it as a question.

- **Framework**: Next.js 15 (App Router), TypeScript strict mode.
- **Styling**: Tailwind CSS + shadcn/ui (Radix primitives) for
  components. No CSS-in-JS. No styled-components.
- **Content**: MDX for marketing pages (so we can compose prose +
  inline components like the calibration chart). The sample brief
  is delivered as MDX too, generated FROM the same brief renderer
  module in the engine repo (port `brief_renderer.py`'s rendering
  logic to a TypeScript module so web + email + PDF stay
  byte-identical).
- **Database**: Postgres via Supabase (free tier sufficient for
  v1). Schemas: `subscribers`, `subscriptions`, `briefs`,
  `outcome_log_public_view`. Migrations via Supabase CLI.
- **Auth**: Supabase Auth — email magic-link only for v1. No
  social login (most Indian retail traders prefer email +
  WhatsApp; OAuth providers add friction without trust).
- **Payments**: Razorpay (NOT Stripe — Stripe doesn't process INR
  one-time + subscription mixed flows as cleanly in India).
  Webhook handler at `/api/razorpay/webhook` to flip the
  subscriber's `tier` field. Pricing TBD by user; build the
  pricing PAGE first, plug in numbers later.
- **Email**: Resend for transactional + brief delivery (free tier
  3K/mo, paid tier ₹X for what we'll need). Templates as React
  Email components. NO Mailchimp / SendGrid.
- **Hosting**: Vercel. Preview deploys on every PR. Production
  on `main`. Domain via Vercel DNS.
- **Analytics**: Plausible (self-hosted on the same Vercel project
  via the `plausible-tracker` lib OR a tiny custom edge endpoint
  writing to Supabase). NO Google Analytics — we are a research
  brand, GA hurts the brand. Track only: page views, conversion
  funnel (landing → sample brief → pricing → signup), brief-read
  rate per subscriber. No personal data, no cross-site.
- **Error tracking**: Sentry, free tier.

## 7. Visual direction

- **Typography**: headlines in Fraunces (serif) — gives "serious
  research" feel. Body in Inter. Mono in JetBrains Mono (used
  only for the calibration tables, not as decoration).
- **Colour**: a dark-mode-first palette. Background `#0E0F11`
  (near-black warm), foreground `#E8E6E1` (warm off-white).
  Accent: `#7C9BB8` (muted steel blue — calm, research-grade,
  not a fintech-blue cliché). Error/drift: `#C66B5C` (muted
  rust, not red). Success/calibrated: `#7DA982` (muted sage,
  not green).
- **Layout**: max content width 720px on long-form pages, 1100px
  on dashboards. Generous whitespace. NO sidebars on marketing
  pages.
- **Imagery**: minimal. Charts (yes), photos (only founder photo
  on /about, optional). NO stock photography, NO illustrations
  of "traders looking at screens", NO abstract gradients. Empty
  space is the design.
- **Charts**: Recharts, all monochromatic with the accent colour
  for series. No multi-colour palettes. Annotations as plain text.
- **Motion**: minimal. Page transitions: 150ms fade. NO scroll-
  triggered animations, NO parallax, NO confetti.
- **Density**: dense like a research note, not airy like a SaaS
  landing page. Customers should feel they are reading a journal,
  not a sales page.

The aesthetic reference is: Stripe.com circa 2018 (before they
went playful), Linear.app's typography, Pitchbook's data density,
the Financial Times's restraint.
